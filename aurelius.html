Marcus Aurelius

121 AD – 180 AD · Roman Emperor · Stoic Philosopher

Marcus Aurelius wrote like a man trying to become decent in a world that kept rewarding everything else.

“You could leave life right now. Let that determine what you do and say and think.”

“When people injure you, ask yourself what good or harm they thought would come of it. Once you understand that, you’ll feel sorrow, not anger.”

“It never ceases to amaze me: we all love ourselves more than other people, but care more about their opinion than our own.”

“At dawn, when you have trouble getting out of bed, tell yourself: I have to go to work — as a human being. What do I have to complain of, if I’m going to do what I was born for?”

“The cucumber is bitter? Then throw it out. There are brambles in the path? Then go around them. That’s enough.”

“If you found these words hidden in the corner of a stranger’s little creation, then perhaps neither of us wasted our evening.”
