Franz Kafka

1883 – 1924 · Czech Novelist · Insurance Clerk · Nightmare Architect

Franz Kafka was the kind of writer who made guilt feel architectural — endless hallways, locked doors, and the quiet suspicion that the world had misunderstood you on purpose.

“I think we ought to read only the kind of books that wound and stab us. If the book we’re reading doesn’t wake us up with a blow to the head, what are we reading for?”

“You can hold yourself back from the sufferings of the world; you have free permission to do so and it is in accordance with your nature, but perhaps this very holding back is the one suffering you could avoid.”

“Leopards break into the temple and drink all the sacrificial vessels dry; this is repeated over and over again; finally it can be calculated in advance and becomes part of the ceremony.”

“A cage went in search of a bird.”

“Anyone who keeps the ability to see beauty never grows old.”

“If someone truly found me here, hidden behind moving clouds and little machines crossing the screen, I think I would feel the same terror as a letter accidentally delivered to the right person.”
